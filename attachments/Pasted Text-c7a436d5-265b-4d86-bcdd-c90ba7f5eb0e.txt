Understanding the Biblical teaching on music in Christian worship

Instrumental Music in Worship
The Biblical Pattern for Music in Worship
Churches of Christ practice a cappella singing (vocal music without instrumental accompaniment) in worship. This practice is based on the New Testament pattern for Christian worship.

Every passage in the New Testament that mentions music in Christian worship specifies singing:

"Speaking to one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord" (Ephesians 5:19)
"Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom, teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord" (Colossians 3:16)
"I will sing with the spirit, and I will also sing with the understanding" (1 Corinthians 14:15)
"Is anyone cheerful? Let him sing psalms" (James 5:13)
Historical Perspective
Instrumental music was not used in Christian worship for the first several centuries of the church's existence. Historical records indicate that early Christians followed the New Testament pattern of a cappella singing.

The introduction of instrumental music into Christian worship came much later and represents a departure from the original practice of the church established by the apostles under the guidance of the Holy Spirit.

Historical Perspective on Church Music
Early Church Practice
Historical evidence indicates that the early church practiced a cappella singing exclusively. Instrumental music was not part of Christian worship for several centuries after the church was established.

Many early Christian writers specifically mentioned that Christians used only vocal music, distinguishing their practice from both Jewish temple worship and pagan ceremonies which used instruments.

Historical Quotes
"The one instrument of peace, the Word alone by which we honor God, is what we employ. We no longer employ the ancient psaltery, trumpet, timbrel, and flute."

- Clement of Alexandria (c. 150-215 AD)

"Musical instruments belong to the Jewish ceremonies and agree with the childish state of the Old Testament."

- John Calvin (Protestant Reformer)

"I have no objection to instruments of music in our chapels, provided they are neither heard nor seen."

- John Wesley (Founder of Methodism)

Introduction of Instruments
The introduction of instrumental music into Christian worship came gradually and faced significant opposition. The organ was first introduced in Western church worship around the 7th century, but was not widely accepted until much later.

Many religious groups during the Restoration Movement of the 19th century sought to return to New Testament patterns of worship, which included a return to a cappella singing in worship services.

Our Commitment to Biblical Worship
At Watkins Road Church of Christ, we are committed to following the New Testament pattern for worship. Our practice of a cappella singing is not merely a tradition or preference, but a deliberate effort to honor God's instructions for Christian worship.

We believe that the beauty of congregational singing allows for the mutual edification described in Ephesians 5:19 and Colossians 3:16, where we are instructed to speak to and teach one another through our songs. Each member participates in this form of worship, making melody in their hearts to the Lord.

While we recognize that instrumental music is pleasing to many, our aim is to please God by adhering to the pattern He has established for His church. We invite you to experience the simple beauty and spiritual depth of congregational a cappella singing in our worship services.
